Cellular Respiration

Cells release the energy stored in glucose in stages. After glycolysis, the process moves into the mitochondria, where the next stage takes place.

The Krebs cycle turns acetyl-CoA into CO2, NADH, FADH2 and ATP.

The electron carriers made here power the final stage, the electron transport chain, which produces most of the cell’s ATP.

Without oxygen at the end of that chain, the whole process stalls — which is why we breathe.
